    <h1>What does <em>+1°C</em> really mean?</h1>
    <p class="intro-lead">For one station in one month, it means the month’s average temperature was 1°C warmer than that station’s average for the <strong>same calendar month</strong> in 1991–2020.</p>
    <div class="intro-equation" aria-label="Example: a typical July average of 20 degrees Celsius and a recent July average of 21 degrees Celsius equals a positive 1 degree Celsius anomaly">
      <div><span>TYPICAL JULY</span><strong>20°C</strong></div>
      <div class="intro-equation-sign">→</div>
      <div><span>A RECENT JULY</span><strong>21°C</strong></div>
      <div class="intro-equation-result"><span>DIFFERENCE</span><strong>+1°C</strong></div>
    </div>
    <div class="intro-explain"><span class="intro-explain-mark" aria-hidden="true">*</span><p>One warm month does not tell the whole story. But repeated positive anomalies show a shift away from the familiar baseline. A warmer average also changes the conditions in which heat extremes can happen. This dashboard lets you inspect the pattern <em>and</em> the coverage behind it.</p></div>
